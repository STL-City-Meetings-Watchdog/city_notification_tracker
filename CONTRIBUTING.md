# Contributing

Thanks for your interest in contributing! This document covers how to contribute, and — importantly — how this project's license can be changed in the future.

## How to Contribute

1. Fork the repository and create a branch for your change.
2. Make your change, with tests where applicable.
3. Open a pull request describing what you changed and why.
4. A maintainer will review and merge if it looks good.

## Licensing of Contributions

By submitting a contribution (via pull request, patch, or any other means) to this project, you agree that:

- Your contribution is licensed to the project under the project's current license (see `COPYING`), and
- You agree to be bound by the **Relicensing Policy** below with respect to that contribution.

You retain copyright over your own contributions. This policy does not transfer copyright to the project or its maintainers — it only governs how future licensing decisions are made.

## Relicensing Policy

Because every contributor holds copyright in their own contributions, changing the project's license in the future requires the agreement of the contributors whose code remains in the codebase. To keep this workable as the project grows — and to avoid getting stuck if someone can't be reached — this project uses the following process.

### Definitions

- **Contributor**: anyone who has submitted a contribution that remains part of the codebase at the time a relicensing proposal is made.
- **Notification**: a message sent to the email address associated with a Contributor's GitHub account, describing the proposed new license and linking to its full text.
- **Responsive Contributor**: a Contributor who replies to a Notification — in any form, agreeing or objecting — within **60 calendar days** of it being sent.
- **Non-Responsive Contributor**: a Contributor who does not reply within that 60-day window. This includes Contributors who are known to be deceased, whose contributions are treated the same way under this policy: as devolving to the Responsive Contributors, per the process below.

### Process

1. A maintainer (or any Contributor) proposes a new license, in writing, stating the reason for the change and linking the exact license text.
2. Maintainers send Notification to every Contributor, using the email address associated with their GitHub account.
3. Maintainers keep a written record of who was notified, when, and how (this can be as simple as a dated list in an issue or internal doc).
4. Contributors have 60 days from the date Notification was sent to respond.
5. After the 60-day window closes:
   - The relicensing decision is made by **unanimous agreement of the Responsive Contributors**.
   - **Non-Responsive Contributors are deemed to have delegated their consent to the decision of the Responsive Contributors.** Their prior contributions may be relicensed under the newly agreed license as part of this process.
6. If any Responsive Contributor objects, the relicensing does not proceed for the codebase as a whole. (Maintainers may still choose to fork, dual-license going forward, or rewrite the objecting contributor's code — but that's a separate decision, not covered by this policy.)

### Keeping Your Contact Info Current

Since this process relies on being able to reach you, please keep a working email address associated with your GitHub account. Notification is sent there and nowhere else — there's no separate contact list to maintain.

### Deceased Contributors

If a Contributor is known to have died, their contributions are treated as devolving to the Responsive Contributors for purposes of this policy — the same outcome as if they were Non-Responsive. This policy doesn't attempt to contact a deceased Contributor's estate or heirs before proceeding.

### Scope

This policy applies to changes of the project's license *after* it has already been established. It does not apply to the initial choice of license, which is made by agreement of the contributors active at that time.
